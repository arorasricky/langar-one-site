What Is Langar?

Langar is the Sikh tradition of the free community kitchen: a shared meal, served by volunteers, open to everyone. It is the idea Langar One is built on.

A free meal for anyone who walks in

Every gurdwara, the Sikh place of worship, has a langar: a kitchen where a free vegetarian meal is cooked and served by volunteers. People of every faith and background sit side by side and eat the same food. Nobody is asked who they are, and nobody is turned away.

Where the tradition comes from

Langar goes back more than 500 years to Guru Nanak, the founder of Sikhism. The third Guru, Guru Amar Das, made it a lasting institution with the rule pehle pangat, pichhe sangat: first eat together, then meet together.

From the community kitchen to the doorstep

Langar One brings this tradition to families who cannot easily come to a shared table, by delivering free groceries to their homes. The principles are the same: everyone is welcome, and every family is served with dignity. See how Langar One works, or sponsor a family's groceries.

Who can eat at a langar?

Anyone. Langar is open to people of every faith, background, and income, and the meal is always free.

How is Langar One connected to langar?

Langar One is inspired by the tradition. Instead of a shared meal in a gurdwara, we deliver free groceries to the homes of families facing food insecurity, with the same principles: everyone is welcome, and everyone is served with dignity.
